# Extended Methods

Supplementary methods for: Opine metabolism genes horizontally transferred into fungal genomes

This document gives the program settings, numerical thresholds and procedural details that complement Section 2 of the article. Section numbers follow the article. The code and the small input files of every step are available in the project repository (https://github.com/pzhurbenko/fungal-opine-genes).

## 2.1. Seed proteins and protein-level screen

The eleven opine synthesis genes of the T-DNA of Ti and Ri plasmids were used as seeds: *sus*, *cus*, *chs*, *acs*, *nos*, *ocs*, *vis*, *ags*, *mas1*, *mas2* and *mis*. For each gene the seed set comprised the protein of a reference plasmid, its variants from other Ti/Ri plasmids and, for *sus*, *cus*, *acs* and *mis*, homologs from the cellular T-DNA (cT-DNA) of naturally transgenic plants; sequences sharing ≥95% identity were collapsed, giving 56 seed proteins (Table S2). A second *acs* seed was taken from pKerr14a, a Ti-type plasmid of *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* strain Kerr 14 (ENA LT009732), whose *acs* falls outside the clade of canonical T-DNA *acs* genes.

All fungal genome assemblies with protein annotation available in NCBI on 5 September 2026 were downloaded with NCBI Datasets [1]: 6,847 assemblies, 6,123 after merging the GenBank and RefSeq versions of the same assembly (18 phyla, 170 orders; 4,512 Ascomycota, 926 Basidiomycota, 685 early-diverging fungi). Each proteome was searched against the seeds with DIAMOND blastp v2.2.6 [2] in very-sensitive mode (E ≤ 10⁻³, bit score ≥ 50), keeping the best hit per proteome and gene.

Each detected protein was then used as a query in a reverse search against NCBI nr (DIAMOND blastp, sensitive mode, E ≤ 10⁻⁵, up to 1,000 targets): all detected proteins for the six genes with homologs in at most 259 proteomes, and 150 per gene (the 50 most similar to the seed and 100 at random) for *nos*, *ocs*, *vis*, *ags* and *mas1*. Hits were assigned to kingdoms and fungal orders with the NCBI Taxonomy. A protein has non-fungal closest relatives when, after exclusion of hits from its own order, the bit score of its best bacterial or plant hit exceeds that of its best fungal hit.

Proteins ≥90% identical to a bacterial protein were regarded as probable contamination of the assembly: 12 of the 1,277 proteins tested were 90.5–100% identical to proteins of soil or fungus-associated bacteria unrelated to *Agrobacterium*, no other protein exceeded 79.1%, and the contigs of the 12 have a GC content 4–28 percentage points above the assembly average and a coding fraction of 0.77–1.0 (Table S5a).

The remaining proteins with non-fungal closest relatives were sorted by three successive rules, all evaluated on hits outside the query's own order (Table 1). Rule A (bacterial-type protein): at least 35 of the 50 best hits are non-fungal and the best bacterial or plant hit outscores the best fungal hit by at least 50 bits. Rule B (T-DNA candidate): a rule-A protein at least 35% identical to a T-DNA seed over at least 40% of the seed length, with identity to the seed at least 0.9 times its identity to the best bacterial hit, so that a canonical T-DNA gene (Section 2.3) is its closest bacterial relative or nearly so; because the seeds include plant cT-DNA proteins, identities to the bacterial seeds alone are given separately in Table S3. Rule C (non-T-DNA candidate): a rule-A protein that fails rule B but whose best bacterial hit, at ≥50% identity, belongs to Rhizobiales. Rules B and C were applied per gene and fungal genus, so that partial or alternative annotations of the same lineage were assigned together. Rule-A proteins passing neither rule were not analysed further. The rules only select candidates; the origin of each lineage was established by phylogeny (Section 2.6).

The thresholds of this and the following sections are collected in Table EM1. Each lies in a gap of the distribution of the corresponding statistic or within a range of values over which the result does not change; the sensitivity of each threshold is given in Table S4 and Figure S1.

## 2.2. Genome-level screen

The genome-level set (2,215 distinct assemblies; Data S3) was assembled independently of the annotated proteomes, by two routes.

(i) Profile screen. A preliminary TBLASTN search [3] of the NCBI WGS database (March 2025) with representatives of the Sus, Cus and GDPD families returned hits in assemblies of various orders; the 1,386 assemblies of orders other than Boletales with hits were screened in full. Because the loci of bacterial type were concentrated in Boletales, all 517 assemblies of this order available on 6 October 2026 were screened regardless of preliminary hits (Table S6). Each assembly was searched by DIAMOND blastx (very-sensitive, frameshift-aware, E ≤ 10⁻⁵) against 388 representative proteins of T-DNA genes, opine-related genes and plasmid maintenance and transfer genes (*vir*, *tra*, *rep*) from the plasmid catalogue (Section 2.3), and all six-frame open reading frames of at least 60 codons were compared with 63 profile HMMs (hmmsearch, HMMER v3.4 [4], E ≤ 10⁻³): one profile per tree-defined group of the three families, one outgroup profile per family built from its closest homologs outside the T-DNA lineages, and 51 profiles of the remaining T-DNA genes. An open reading frame was assigned to a lineage when its best lineage profile outscored the outgroup profile by at least 20 bits with a score of at least 30. The nopaline synthase-related genes of Boletales were detected by the blastx component (Table S10b). Two assemblies with *cus*-like genes found only in the protein-level screen (*Hebeloma velutipes*, *Monosporozyma servazzii*) were added; their gene models were taken from the NCBI annotation.

(ii) Targeted scans of *Fusarium* and related genera. The *chs*-like genes and the *odh* island were searched directly in 324 assemblies of *Fusarium*, *Neocosmospora* and *Dactylonectria* (Table S7) by aligning the reference proteins to each genome with miniprot [5]. A *chs*-like locus was retained at ≥45% identity to chrysopine synthase (native fungal M24 aminopeptidases reach at most 37.4%, the *chs*-like lineage at least 44.0%) or, for the truncated copies and pseudogenes of *F. oxysporum*, at ≥70% identity to the *F. vanettenii* protein. An *odh* island locus was retained at ≥60% identity to the proteins of *F. poae* Fp133 (KAL3595992.1–KAL3595994.1; native homologs ≤40%). Loci were classified as in Section 2.4.

## 2.3. Reference plasmid catalogue and control of bacterial contamination

A catalogue of 42 complete Ti and Ri plasmids (7,105 protein-coding sequences) was assembled from NCBI. T-DNA border repeats were located by scanning each plasmid for 25-mers matching the border consensus at ≥18 of 25 positions; T-regions could be delimited on 15 plasmids, and their 302 proteins were clustered with MMseqs2 [6] (50% identity, 70% coverage) into 51 clusters. The genes of these T-regions together with the eleven seed genes are referred to as the canonical T-DNA. pKerr14a is not part of the catalogue: on this draft assembly *acs* lies 1.6 kb from a single border-like repeat with no second border within 60 kb, so whether it belongs to a T-region is undetermined. The catalogue served to build the profiles of the remaining T-DNA genes and to detect bacterial contigs; it was not used to classify fungal proteins.

Fungal contigs with blastx hits to the 142 *vir*, *tra* and *rep* proteins of the 388-protein set at ≥60% identity were flagged as possible contamination by Ti/Ri plasmid DNA; the threshold is lower than the 90% of Section 2.1 because the flag is placed on a contig, not on a gene, and sensitivity matters more. No contig carrying a locus of the catalogue is flagged at any threshold between 40% and 80% (Tables S4, S5b). The ±50-kb windows around the *Rhypophila* opine dehydrogenase genes, which were outside the profile screen, were compared with the plasmid proteins in the same way.

## 2.4. Gene models and the catalogue of loci

For each detected locus the entire contig was downloaded (for contigs longer than 1 Mb, a window of ±100 kb) and a gene model was built by alignment of the query protein to the genome with splicing awareness (miniprot v0.18 [5]) and by de novo prediction (AUGUSTUS v3.1 [7], *Laccaria bicolor* parameters). A locus was classified as intact (at least 90% of the reference protein covered, no stop codons or frameshifts), pseudogene (reading frame disrupted), truncated (reading frame intact, coverage below 90%) or fragment (too short to be placed in a tree). A preliminary lineage assignment used competitive HMM profiles (the profile of each lineage against the profiles of its closest relatives, Section 2.2); loci that matched a profile of the relatives better than any lineage profile, or no profile, were classified as not assigned to any lineage and are listed separately in Table S1b. Short fragments related to the pKerr14a-type lineage were assigned to it when they shared at least 60% identity with one of its six intact members (DIAMOND blastp); identities of the candidate fragments show a gap between 49.4% and 62.7%. Gene models of the nopaline synthase-related loci of Boletales were built with miniprot using the annotated protein of *Butyriboletus roseoflavus* (KAG8213183.1) as reference.

Loci from all entry routes were merged into a single catalogue (Table S1b; 2,864 loci), in which the route of each locus is recorded, and summarised by lineage in Table 2. The 254 loci of the seven lineages (Table S1a) comprise 239 loci placed on the trees directly or through a near-identical representative, 13 pKerr14a-type fragments assigned by identity and two *cus*-like loci added from NCBI annotations after tree inference. A *lineage* is an operational group of fungal loci with the same closest relatives in the tree of their protein family; a *carrier* is an assembly with at least one locus assigned to a lineage.

## 2.5. Tests against bacterial contamination

Three tests were applied in addition to the search for plasmid maintenance genes (Section 2.3); the evidence for each lineage is summarised in Table S18.

Neighbouring genes: the contig length and the genes on either side of each locus were recorded, and the neighbours were classified by their best hits in nr (Section 2.7); the flanking genes of the *odh* island of *F. poae* Fp133 were searched against nr directly. Independent assemblies: contigs with the genes were aligned in full (minimap2 v2.30 [8], asm20 preset) to other assemblies of the same species, and the coverage of the locus and of its 2-kb flanks was recorded (Table S9; Boletales only). Sequencing reads: for seven carrier genomes with Illumina paired-end reads (BioProject PRJNA1022813; 23–216× coverage), reads were mapped to the whole assembly with minimap2 (short-read preset) and processed with SAMtools v1.24 [9]; for each locus we recorded the depth relative to the genome-wide median, the reads spanning each gene boundary by at least 40 bp, the fraction of properly oriented pairs and the mapping quality (Table S8).

The tests were combined into one status per locus by fixed rules, applied in order. Confirmed: a fungal gene on both sides of the locus, or the locus and both flanks covered ≥0.5 by an independent assembly of the species, or a read-depth ratio of 0.5–2 with junction reads on both flanks, or location on an assembled chromosome at ≥1 kb from both ends. Not assessable: a contig shorter than the locus plus 2 kb, or a locus touching a contig end. Supported: a gene of the same lineage in two or more assemblies of the species, or a contig of ≥100 kb with the locus away from its ends and no bacterial context, or a fungal gene on one side only, or a genome-like depth without junction reads on both sides. All other loci are not assessable. Of the 254 loci, 94 are confirmed, 88 supported and 72 not assessable (Tables S1a, S1d).

## 2.6. Phylogenetic trees, lineage assignment and AU tests

For each family a dataset was assembled of fungal gene models (intact and pseudogenes), canonical T-DNA references, other bacterial homologs, native fungal homologs and an outgroup of distant members of the superfamily. Sequences were trimmed to the common domain and aligned with MAFFT v7.526 (L-INS-i) [10]; the alignment was trimmed with ClipKIT v2.7.0 [11] in smart-gap mode, and sequences retaining less than 15% of the alignment positions were removed. Trees were inferred with IQ-TREE v3.1.1 [12] under the model selected by ModelFinder [13] (Q.PFAM+R5 for the OCD-fold family, JTT+R5 for the GDPD family, JTT+R7 for the cucumopine synthase family; Q.PFAM+I+G4 for the *chs*-like dataset, Q.PFAM+G4 for the *odh* island, Q.PFAM+R4 for the nopaline synthase subfamily), with SH-aLRT [14] and ultrafast bootstrap [15] support from 1,000 replicates. The Sus, Cus and GDPD trees were rooted on distant superfamily members, the *chs*-like tree on the T-DNA *ags* seeds and the opine dehydrogenase trees on the *ocs* and *vis* seeds. The *chs*-like, *odh*-island and nopaline-subfamily datasets comprised the fungal proteins, their best bacterial hits in nr (up to two or three per genus), the relevant T-DNA seeds and native fungal homologs from several classes. The GDPD protein of *Pleurotus* was analysed on the GDPD dataset extended by its best bacterial hit and five native Agaricomycete GDPDs, aligned to the family profile with hmmalign (Q.PFAM+G4 and, as a control, VT+I+R4). A second alignment of each dataset generated with a profile HMM gave the same conclusions. Trees, trimmed alignments and IQ-TREE reports are provided as Data S2; the full trees are shown in Figures S2–S7.

Each locus was assigned to a lineage by its position in the tree: to the *sus*-like or *cus*-like lineage when it fell within the smallest clade containing all canonical T-DNA references of the gene, and to another lineage when it fell within the clade of that lineage; near-identical sequences collapsed before tree inference received the lineage of their representative. A group of fungal loci was counted as a lineage of bacterial origin when it groups with bacterial homologs, the alternative placements tested are rejected by the AU test where the test is informative, and at least one independent line of evidence supports the placement (a bacterial source locus, nesting within the T-DNA clade, location in islands with other genes of bacterial type, or a patchy distribution among related genomes).

Alternative placements were tested with the approximately unbiased (AU) test [16]: the best tree under each constraint was found with IQ-TREE and compared with the unconstrained tree using 10,000 RELL replicates; p < 0.05 rejects the constraint. For each lineage the constraints forced the fungal genes to group (i) with the T-DNA references of the gene, (ii) with other bacterial homologs, (iii) with the native fungal homologs of the family and (iv) with each other; in addition, the fungal *chs*-like genes were placed next to each of the five Ti-plasmid *chs* genes, the *cus*-like genes outside Boletales (16 sequences of 10 species) were placed with the T-DNA references excluding the Boletales genes, and the *Rhypophila* genes were grouped with the native opine dehydrogenases of Sordariomycetes and of all fungi. The 60 constraints with their p-values are listed in Table S11 and Figure S8, the alternative tested by each in Table S11b, and the constraint trees in Data S2b; adjustment of the p-values by the Holm procedure within each family left every verdict unchanged. The number of independent *cus*-like groups outside Boletales was counted as the minimal number of clades consisting only of these genes, before and after collapsing nodes with SH-aLRT < 80 or UFBoot < 95. Constraints that force the candidates together with all native fungal members of a family are informative only where that native set is monophyletic in the unconstrained tree; where it is not (the OCD-fold, GDPD and opine dehydrogenase families), the conclusion rests on the support of the node joining the fungal genes to their bacterial relatives, on the constraints among bacterial groups and on the genomic context (Tables S11b, S18).

As an independent line of evidence for the GDPD family, structures of 274 proteins were predicted with ColabFold v1.6.2 [17,18], aligned structurally with FoldMason [19] and used to build a tree with IQ-TREE, which confirmed the monophyly of the acc-type lineage, the pKerr14a-type lineage and the T-DNA references (Figure S9).

## 2.7. Genomic context and islands

For each locus a context window was delimited along the contig: moving outwards in both directions, the first gene on each side that looks like an ordinary gene of the carrier genome, that is, a multi-exon gene whose best homologs in nr are fungal, was taken as an anchor. The region between the anchors was called an island when it contains more than one gene; otherwise the locus is reported as a single gene. The window describes the local organisation of the locus, not the boundaries of the transferred DNA. Genes within islands were classified by their best homologs in nr as fungal, bacterial-like or mobile elements, and the GC content of each island was calculated. To test whether an island is an insertion, its anchors were searched with miniprot in genomes of close relatives lacking the gene. The 163 proteins encoded in 17 islands were compared with the plasmid catalogue (DIAMOND blastp, E < 10⁻⁵) to identify other genes of plasmid origin and to compare gene order with that of T-regions (Table S12). For the *odh* island of *F. poae*, the proteins within 50 kb of each copy were searched in the same way, and the locus of their best bacterial hit was examined in the annotated genome of *Agrobacterium larrymoorei* SORGH_AS_0974 (GCF_031453325.1).

## 2.8. Compositional analysis

For the 75 intact genes of the *sus*-like, *cus*-like, acc-type GDPD and pKerr14a-type lineages (four coding sequences with ambiguous bases or a length not divisible by three were excluded) and for the six nos-subfamily genes of Boletales, the GC content of the coding sequence, the GC content at third codon positions (GC3) and the relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) were compared with those of carrier genes. The background comprised all coding sequences of the genome for annotated assemblies and, for unannotated assemblies, the genes predicted around the loci; because an own-genome background of at least 30 genes was available for only 24 of the 75 loci, a pooled background of 1,001 Boletales genes was used in addition. Genes of mobile elements, coding sequences shorter than 300 nt and the loci themselves were excluded. For each gene we determined the percentile of its GC3 within the background and the distance of its codon usage from the background (one minus the Pearson correlation of the RSCU vectors), compared with background genes of similar length (Table S14). GC3 of the *odh* island genes was compared with the 14,418 annotated genes of *F. poae* Fp133, and the GC content of the *chs*-like genes with that of their carrier genomes.

## 2.9. Test for selection (dN/dS)

For the non-redundant intact genes of the T-DNA clades (31 *sus*-like and 27 *cus*-like sequences) codon alignments were built by aligning the proteins with MAFFT and back-translating to codons, and trees were inferred with IQ-TREE (GTR+G). The codeml program of PAML v4.10.7 [20] was used to fit model M0 and the site models M7 and M8 (likelihood ratio test), and yn00 [21] for pairwise estimates; both a strict alignment (codon columns without gaps) and one with columns of ≤20% gaps were analysed. The same analysis was applied to the intact copies of the other lineages (Table S15): *chs*-like genes (6 sequences), *cus*-like genes outside Boletales (20 sequences of 12 species, and one per species), pKerr14a-type genes (6), nos-subfamily genes of Boletales (4) and the three genes of the *odh* island (5, 2 and 3 sequences; with the M8a null model). Where synonymous sites were saturated (median pairwise dS > 2) the M0 estimate was used and the median over pairs with dS ≤ 2 is reported in addition. The M0 estimates pool branches before and after transfer; no branch model was fitted. Four sets were recomputed after excluding the loci whose integration is not assessable (Section 2.5). All codon alignments, trees and codeml and yn00 outputs are provided as Data S4.

## 2.10. Expression analysis (RNA-seq)

The SRA [22] was searched for RNA-seq data of the carrier genera. For Boletales the only datasets found were for *Boletus edulis* (SRR9650682, SRR9650679) and *Butyriboletus subappendiculatus* (ERR13363456), neither from a sequenced carrier isolate; reads were mapped with splicing awareness (minimap2) to the carrier assembly of the same species (*B. edulis* GCA_038093215.1) or of the closest carrier (*B. roseoflavus* GCA_019364595.1), and read counts at the loci were compared with the median over the genes of the genome (Table S16). For the *chs*-like genes nine public datasets of *F. vanettenii* 77-13-4 and *F. oxysporum* f. sp. *lycopersici* 4287 were used, and for the *odh* island 15 datasets of the carrier strain *F. poae* Fp133 (BioProject PRJNA924782): the first 10 million read pairs (*F. vanettenii* and *F. oxysporum*) or reads (*F. poae*) of each run were mapped with minimap2 in spliced mode, and reads overlapping the exons of each annotated gene were counted and normalised to gene length and to the total over all genes. Only primary alignments with mapping quality ≥1 were counted, so reads mapping equally well to the two copies of the *odh* island were not assigned to a copy, and transcription is shown for the island as a whole.

## 2.11. Species clusters of Boletales

Relatedness of the 517 Boletales assemblies was evaluated with Mash v2.3 [23] (sketch size 20,000, k = 21), which gives an estimate of average nucleotide identity (ANI) from k-mer sets. Assemblies with ANI >97% (Mash distance <0.03) were grouped into species-level clusters, defined as connected components (358 clusters), which replace the NCBI species labels in all counts of carrier species.
