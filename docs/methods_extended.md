# Opine metabolism genes horizontally transferred into fungal genomes

This document is the full, parameter-level description of the methods behind the article; section numbers follow Section 2 of the article; it accompanies the code in this repository.

### 2.1. Seed proteins and protein-level screen

Eleven opine synthesis genes encoded by the T-DNA of Ti and Ri plasmids were used as seeds: *sus* (succinamopine synthase), *cus* (cucumopine synthase), *chs* (chrysopine synthase), *acs* (agrocinopine synthase), *nos* (nopaline synthase), *ocs* (octopine synthase), *vis* (vitopine synthase), *ags* (agropine synthase), *mas1* and *mas2* (mannopine synthesis) and *mis* (mikimopine synthase). For each gene the seed set comprised the protein of a reference plasmid, its variants from other Ti/Ri plasmids and, for *sus*, *cus*, *acs* and *mis*, homologs from the cellular T-DNA (cT-DNA) of naturally transgenic plants [16]; sequences sharing ≥95% identity were collapsed, giving 56 seed proteins (Table S2).

For *acs*, a second seed was taken from pKerr14a, a Ti-type plasmid of *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* str. Kerr 14 (ENA LT009732; the genome of strain Kerr 14 is described by Lassalle et al. 2017, Genome Biol. Evol. 9, 3413) whose *acs* gene falls outside the clade of canonical T-DNA *acs* genes (WP_280174108; the canonical T-DNA is defined in Section 2.3); it is reported separately as "*acs* (pKerr14a)" in Figure 2 and Table 1.

All fungal genome assemblies with protein annotation available in NCBI on 5 September 2026 were downloaded with NCBI Datasets [M1] (6,847 assemblies; 6,123 after merging GenBank and RefSeq versions of the same assembly; "genome" and "assembly" are used interchangeably below, and a proteome is an annotated assembly). These 6,123 proteomes represent 18 phyla, 63 classes, 170 orders and 970 genera of fungi (NCBI Taxonomy), dominated by Ascomycota (4,512 proteomes) and Basidiomycota (926), with 685 proteomes of early-diverging groups (Kickxellomycota, Mucoromycota, Mortierellomycota, Microsporidia, Chytridiomycota, Glomeromycota and others). Each proteome was searched against the seed set with DIAMOND blastp v2.2.6 [M2] in very-sensitive mode (E ≤ 10⁻³, bit score ≥ 50), and the best hit per genome and gene was retained.

To determine whether a fungal protein detected in this way belongs to a family native to fungi, it was used as a query in a reverse search against the NCBI non-redundant protein database (nr), not against the genome assemblies (DIAMOND blastp, sensitive mode, E ≤ 10⁻⁵, up to 1,000 targets). The number of proteins tested depended on the number of proteomes in which homologs of a gene had been detected: for *sus*, *cus*, *chs*, *acs*, *mas2* and *mis*, with homologs in at most 259 proteomes, every detected protein was tested; for *nos*, *ocs*, *vis*, *ags* and *mas1*, with homologs in 733–6,045 proteomes, 150 proteins per gene were tested — the 50 most similar to the seed and 100 chosen at random. Hits were assigned to kingdoms and fungal orders using the NCBI Taxonomy (taxdump of 2026-10-06). Organism names are taken from the trailing bracket(s) of each nr title; names that match the taxdump only after truncation to the binomial or genus, and single-word names, are resolved instead by the taxid of the hit protein itself (NCBI esummary), because nr titles are older than the taxdump and truncated names can hit homonyms (for example, the bacterial genus *Bosea*, now *Allobosea*, versus the plant genus *Bosea*; scripts 02_taxonomy.py and 02b_taxonomy_fix.py, log taxonomy_fix_log.tsv). A protein was considered to have non-fungal closest relatives if, after exclusion of hits from its own order, the bit score of the best bacterial or plant hit exceeded that of the best fungal hit.

Proteins ≥90% identical to a bacterial protein were regarded as probable contamination of the assembly: 12 of the 1,277 proteins tested were 90.5–100% identical to proteins of soil or fungus-associated bacteria unrelated to *Agrobacterium* (none rhizobial) while only 22–41% identical to the seeds, and no other protein exceeded 79.1% identity to a bacterial protein, so the same proteins are classified as contaminants at any threshold between 80% and 90% (Table S4). Eleven of the 12 are among the proteins with non-fungal closest relatives and are counted as contaminants in Table 1; the twelfth, a *chs*-like protein of *Rhizoctonia solani* (CAE6530501.1, 96.4% identical to a *Paenibacillus* protein), has a fungal hit outside its own order that scores higher than the bacterial hit and is therefore not among the proteins with non-fungal closest relatives. The contigs carrying the 11 contaminants carry no Ti/Ri plasmid *vir*/*tra*/*rep* genes, and their bacterial nature is supported by a GC content 4–28 percentage points above the assembly average (all but the 2-kb contig of *Hortaea werneckii*) and a coding fraction of 0.77–1.0, against a median of 0.55 on fungal contigs carrying loci of the catalogue (Table S5a).

The remaining proteins with non-fungal closest relatives were sorted by three successive rules, all evaluated on hits outside the query's own order (Table 1). Rule A (bacterial-type protein): at least 35 of the 50 best hits are non-fungal and the best bacterial or plant hit outscores the best fungal hit by at least 50 bits; proteins with a non-fungal best hit that failed rule A were treated as members of fungal families. Rule B (T-DNA candidate; "T-DNA-type" in earlier versions): a rule-A protein that is at least 35% identical to a T-DNA seed over at least 40% of the seed length, with identity to the seed at least 0.9 times its identity to the best bacterial hit — that is, a protein for which a canonical T-DNA gene (Section 2.3) is the closest bacterial relative or nearly so (within 10% of the identity to the best bacterial hit). Because the seed set includes plant cT-DNA proteins (Section 2.1), the identity used in rule B is identity to the nearest T-DNA seed of the same gene, bacterial or plant cT-DNA, and is not equivalent to identity to the bacterial T-DNA genes; identities of each rule-B protein to the bacterial seeds alone are given separately (Table S3, column identity_to_bacterial_seed_pct; calculated with DIAMOND blastp against 30 bacterial T-DNA seeds of the same gene, Table S17b). Rule C (non-T-DNA candidate, i.e. related to a rhizobial gene outside the canonical T-DNA): a rule-A protein that fails rule B but whose best bacterial hit, at ≥50% identity, belongs to Rhizobiales (Hyphomicrobiales). Rules B and C were applied per gene and fungal genus: when one protein of a genus passed, the other rule-A proteins of the same gene and genus (partial or alternative annotations of the same lineage) were assigned with it. Rule-A proteins passing neither B nor C were recorded as bacterial-type proteins of unrelated families and were not analysed further. The fate of every protein with non-fungal closest relatives is listed in Table S3.

*Choice of thresholds.* The sensitivity of every threshold used to classify proteins and loci was examined by recomputing the result over a range of values (Table S4; Figure S1, `analysis/thresholds/thresholds_distributions.png`). Each threshold either lies in a gap of the distribution of the corresponding statistic or within a range of values over which the result does not change. Three borderline cases are named explicitly: the *sus* homolog of *Melanogaster broomeanus* passes rule A with 37 of the 50 best hits non-fungal and rule B with a seed coverage of 0.497; the GDPD protein of *Pleurotus pulmonarius* (detected with *acs*) passes rule C with 51.0% identity to its best rhizobial hit (*Ensifer*); and the *nos* homolog of *Rhypophila* passes rule B with 36.0% identity to the seed and a seed-to-bacterial identity ratio of 0.911 (it would fail at any threshold above 36.0% or at a ratio above 0.911). Rules A–C select candidates for detailed analysis; the origin of each lineage is determined by phylogenetic analysis (Section 2.6; Results 3.4), not by the rules.

### 2.2. Genome-level screen

The protein-level screen has three limitations, and the search was therefore repeated on genome assemblies. First, annotated proteomes lack pseudogenes and often miss intronless genes, which is the expected structure of a gene recently acquired from bacteria. Second, they do not show the genomic context of a gene, which is needed to exclude contamination and to characterise the insertion. Third, only a minority of the available assemblies are annotated, so the range of carrier fungi of a lineage cannot be estimated from proteomes alone.

Genome assemblies entered the analysis by two routes (Figure 1). The genome-level set (2,215 distinct assemblies; Data S3) is not a subset of the 6,123 annotated proteomes of Section 2.1: it was assembled independently, from a preliminary WGS search and from the complete set of Boletales assemblies.

(i) Profile screen. Two sets of assemblies were analysed. First, a preliminary TBLASTN search [M24] of the NCBI WGS database (March 2025) with representatives of the three protein families that had yielded T-DNA candidates (Sus, Cus and GDPD) returned hits in assemblies of various fungal orders; the 1,386 assemblies of orders other than Boletales with hits were screened in full. Second, because the loci of bacterial type found by this preliminary search were concentrated in Boletales, all 517 assemblies of the order available in NCBI on 6 October 2026 (Table S6) were screened regardless of preliminary hits. Each assembly was screened in two ways: by DIAMOND blastx in very-sensitive mode (frameshift-aware, E ≤ 10⁻⁵) against 388 representative proteins of T-DNA genes, opine-related genes and plasmid maintenance and transfer genes (*vir*, *tra*, *rep*), taken from the Ti/Ri plasmid catalogue (Section 2.3) and therefore without plant cT-DNA proteins; and by hmmsearch (HMMER v3.4 [M3], E ≤ 10⁻³) of all six-frame open reading frames of at least 60 codons against 63 profile HMMs — one profile per tree-defined group of the three families (the lineage profiles), family-specific outgroup profiles, and 51 profiles built from the protein clusters of the remaining T-DNA genes (Section 2.3). The lineage profiles of the three families were built from sets that include most of the seed proteins of Section 2.1, bacterial and plant cT-DNA (36 of 56), whereas the profiles of the remaining T-DNA genes, like the blastx queries, were built from proteins of the plasmid catalogue without plant cT-DNA proteins. The outgroup profile of each family was built from its closest homologs outside the T-DNA lineages: chromosomal bacterial homologs (for example, chromosomal ornithine cyclodeaminases of *Agrobacterium* for the OCD-fold family) and, for GDPD, native fungal glycerophosphodiester phosphodiesterases (Gde1p of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and two *Aspergillus* proteins). An open reading frame was assigned to a lineage when its best lineage profile outscored the outgroup profile by at least 20 bits with a score of at least 30.

Opine dehydrogenase-like genes of Boletales related to nopaline synthase were detected by the blastx component of this screen (hits to the *nos* proteins of the 388-protein set; Table S10b).

Two assemblies with *cus*-like genes found in the protein-level screen but not included in either set (*Hebeloma velutipes* GCA_058411305.1 and *Monosporozyma servazzii* GCA_964275055.1) were added, screened as described above and counted among the 1,386 assemblies of other orders (Data S3); their gene models were taken from the NCBI annotation.

(ii) Targeted scans of *Fusarium* and related genera. Candidates of rules B and C from *Fusarium* (*chs*-like genes and the opine dehydrogenase, *odh*, of *F. langsethiae*) belong to families that the profile screen did not cover, and *Fusarium* is represented by many assemblies; these genes were therefore searched directly in 324 genome assemblies of *Fusarium*, *Neocosmospora* and *Dactylonectria*. For *chs*-like genes, the chrysopine synthase of *Agrobacterium* and the homologous proteins of *F. vanettenii* and *F. oxysporum* were aligned to each genome with miniprot [M5]. A locus was retained if it shared at least 45% identity with chrysopine synthase (native fungal M24 aminopeptidases are at most 37.4% identical, proteins of the *chs*-like lineage at least 44.0%) or, for the truncated copies and pseudogenes of *F. oxysporum* whose identity to the bacterial protein has dropped, at least 70% with the *F. vanettenii* protein; the same loci are retained over wide ranges of both thresholds (Table S4). For the *odh* island, the three proteins of *F. poae* Fp133 (KAL3595992.1, KAL3595993.1 and KAL3595994.1) were aligned to the same 324 genomes; a locus was retained at ≥60% identity to the reference protein (native fungal homologs of the family are ≤40% identical). Loci were classified as in Section 2.4.

### 2.3. Reference plasmid catalogue and control of bacterial contamination

A catalogue of 42 complete Ti and Ri plasmids (7,105 protein-coding sequences) was assembled from NCBI. T-DNA border repeats were located with a script that scanned each plasmid for 25-mers matching the border consensus at ≥18 of 25 positions; T-regions could be delimited in this way on 15 of the 42 plasmids, and the 302 proteins encoded within them were clustered (50% identity, 70% coverage; MMseqs2 [M4]) into 51 protein clusters. The genes of these 15 T-regions, together with the eleven seed genes of Section 2.1, are referred to as the canonical T-DNA; pKerr14a, a draft assembly, is not part of the catalogue, and on it *acs* lies 1.6 kb downstream of a single border-like repeat (20 of 25 positions of the consensus) with no second border within 60 kb, so whether it belongs to a T-region is undetermined. Plasmid proteins were further labelled as T-DNA, opine-related, or plasmid maintenance and transfer genes (the plasmid backbone: *vir*, *tra*, *rep*). Because the T-region annotation is incomplete, the catalogue was not used to classify fungal proteins (rules A–C, Section 2.1, rely on the reverse search against nr); it served to build the profiles of the remaining T-DNA genes (Section 2.2) and to detect bacterial contigs.

Fungal contigs carrying plasmid maintenance and transfer genes (*vir*, *tra* or *rep*) at ≥60% amino acid identity were flagged as possible bacterial contamination. This threshold is lower than the 90% threshold of Section 2.1 because the two serve different purposes: the 90% threshold classifies a single protein as a contaminant, whereas this search looks for whole bacterial contigs using dozens of *vir*/*tra*/*rep* genes at once, so sensitivity matters more than specificity, and the flag is placed on the contig rather than on an individual gene. This rule detects contamination by Ti/Ri plasmids specifically: the assemblies were searched by blastx against the 388-protein set of Section 2.2, of which 142 are *vir*, *tra* and *rep* proteins, and only hits to these count. The genome-wide distribution of identity to these genes is continuous (1,401, 427 and 156 contigs at ≥50%, ≥60% and ≥70%), but the conclusions do not depend on the threshold: no contig carrying a locus of the catalogue is flagged at any threshold between 40% and 80%, and among Boletales *vir*/*tra*/*rep* genes are found only in *Boletus harrisonii* at any threshold between 55% and 89% (Table S4). The 11 contaminants of Section 2.1 are not detected by this rule (their contigs carry no *vir*/*tra*/*rep* genes; Table S5a); they were identified by the reverse search against nr. For the *Rhypophila* opine dehydrogenase genes, which were not part of the profile screen, the ±50-kb windows around the gene in the three assemblies (contigs of 62–97 kb, so that the windows were truncated by contig ends) were searched in the same way: the 87 proteins predicted in the windows were compared by DIAMOND blastp with the 7,105 plasmid proteins of the 42 plasmids, and the windows themselves by blastx.

### 2.4. Gene models and the catalogue of loci

For each detected locus, the entire contig was downloaded (for contigs longer than 1 Mb, a window of ±100 kb) and a gene model was built in two ways: alignment of the query protein to the genome with splicing awareness (miniprot v0.18 [M5]) and de novo prediction (AUGUSTUS v3.1 [M6], *Laccaria bicolor* parameters). Throughout the catalogue a locus was classified as intact (at least 90% of the reference protein covered, no stop codons or frameshifts), pseudogene (reading frame disrupted by stop codons or frameshifts), truncated (reading frame intact, coverage below 90%) or fragment (too short to be placed in a tree; remnants of a few dozen codons are included here); "copy" denotes one locus of a lineage in one assembly. A preliminary lineage assignment was performed using competitive HMM profiles (one profile per lineage plus the profiles of its closest relatives outside it, Section 2.2).

Loci that matched a profile of the relatives better than any lineage profile, or matched no profile, were classified as not assigned to any lineage (distant members of the same superfamily or native fungal genes) and are listed separately in Table S1b.

Short fragments related to the pKerr14a-type lineage, too short to be placed in the tree, were assigned to the lineage when they shared at least 60% identity with one of its six intact members (DIAMOND blastp). Identities of the candidate fragments show a gap between 49.4% (unassigned) and 62.7% (assigned), and the same 13 fragments are assigned at any threshold between 50% and 60% (Table S4).

Gene models of the nopaline synthase-related loci of Boletales were obtained with miniprot using the annotated protein of *Butyriboletus roseoflavus* (KAG8213183.1) as reference; for *Rhypophila*, the annotated proteins were used. Loci from all entry routes — the profile screen, the targeted scans of *Fusarium*, the blastx screen of Boletales and the protein-level screen — were merged into a single catalogue (Table S1b; 2,864 loci), in which the route by which each locus entered is recorded, and summarised by lineage in Table 2. The 254 loci of the seven lineages (Table S1a) comprise 239 loci placed on the trees directly or through a near-identical representative, 13 pKerr14a-type fragments assigned by identity and two *cus*-like loci added from NCBI annotations after tree inference.

Throughout, a *lineage* is an operational group of fungal loci with the same closest relatives in the tree of their protein family; it is not a count of genes or of transfer events (the three genes of the *odh* island form one lineage; the *cus*-like genes outside Boletales comprise several independent acquisitions). A *carrier* is an assembly with at least one locus assigned to a lineage.

### 2.5. Tests against bacterial contamination

A gene of bacterial origin found in a fungal assembly may either be part of the fungal genome or derive from bacterial DNA that contaminated the sequenced sample. Three independent tests were used to distinguish between these possibilities; together with the search for plasmid maintenance genes (Section 2.3), they were applied to every lineage (Table S18; Table 3 of the main text gives the lineages in words).

Neighbouring genes. A contaminating bacterial fragment is expected to sit on a short contig together with other bacterial genes, whereas an integrated gene is flanked by fungal genes on a fungal-sized contig. For each locus we recorded the contig length and the genes on either side of the candidate and classified them by their best hits in nr (Section 2.7); for the *odh* island, the two flanking fungal genes of *F. poae* Fp133 were searched against nr (DIAMOND blastp, sensitive mode, up to 1,000 targets); the miniprot scan of the 324 *Fusarium*-related assemblies (Section 2.2) was performed for the three island genes only.

Independent assemblies. If a species was sequenced independently several times, a contaminant is unlikely to reappear in another assembly at the same place and with the same fungal neighbours, whereas a genuine gene would. Contigs with the genes were aligned in full (minimap2 v2.30 [M19], asm20 mode) to other assemblies of the same species; coverage of the locus and 2-kb flanks was assessed (Table S9; Boletales only). Species identity of the assemblies compared in this way was established by genome-wide similarity (Section 2.11).

Sequencing reads. Contamination typically yields a read depth different from that of the fungal genome and no read pairs connecting the gene with fungal DNA; an integrated gene has genome-like depth and is spanned by reads that continue into the fungal flanks. For seven carrier genomes with available sequencing reads (Illumina paired-end, BioProject PRJNA1022813; 23–216× coverage), reads were mapped to the whole assembly with minimap2 (short-read preset) and processed with SAMtools v1.24 [M20]. For each locus we calculated the depth of coverage relative to the genome-wide median, the number of reads physically spanning each boundary of the gene (at least 40 bp on either side), the fraction of properly oriented pairs, and mapping quality. Reads were not examined for the *odh* island.

Integration status of each locus. The results of these tests were combined into one status per locus by fixed rules (script paper/tables/integration_status.py; per-locus rule tags in integration_status.tsv; column integration_status of Table S1a). A locus gets the first matching class. Confirmed = any of F2, a gene classified as fungal on both sides of the locus (per-neighbour classification from analysis/loci_pipeline/round2/neighbors_class.tsv, majority kingdom Fungi with the top hit from a genus other than the carrier's, or role Fungi in analysis/islands/islands_genes.tsv, which overrides the former; genes overlapping a registered locus are ignored; the flanking genes of the *F. poae* Fp133 *odh* island were classified by their own reverse search; *chs*-like loci have no per-neighbour classification and get no F2); R, the locus and both flanks covered ≥0.5 by an independent assembly of the species (Table S9); D2, read-depth ratio 0.5–2 with junction reads on both flanks (Table S8); CHR, the locus on an assembled-molecule sequence with Assigned-Molecule-Location/Type = Chromosome in the NCBI assembly report, ≥1 kb from both ends (unlocalised and unplaced scaffolds get no CHR). Uncertain (not assessable; applied next) = SHORT, a contig shorter than the locus plus 2 kb, or EDGE, a locus touching a contig end. Supported = any of R2, a gene of the same lineage in two or more assemblies of the species without verification of the flanks (Tables S13a, S13b); LONG, a contig ≥100 kb with the locus away from its ends, no bacterial context and a depth ratio not outside 0.5–2; F1, a fungal gene on one side only (where no per-side data exist, genomic_context "fungal context" or "mixed" of Table S1a counts as F1); D1, depth ratio 0.5–2 without junction reads on both sides. The remaining loci are uncertain (DEPTH, a depth ratio outside 0.5–2 without other support; NONB, no neighbour information and no other support). Result: 94 confirmed, 88 supported, 72 uncertain of 254 loci (Table S1d gives the lineage counts without the uncertain loci). These rules replace an earlier version (v14) in which S1a genomic_context counted as two flanks, R2 counted as confirmed and chromosome-level assembly status counted as CHR.

### 2.6. Phylogenetic trees, lineage assignment and AU tests

The term lineage is defined in Section 2.4. For each family, a dataset was assembled comprising fungal models (intact and pseudogenes), canonical T-DNA references, other bacterial homologs, native fungal homologs and an outgroup of distant members of the superfamily. Sequences were trimmed to the common domain, aligned with MAFFT v7.526 (L-INS-i) [M7], and the alignment was trimmed with ClipKIT v2.7.0 [M8] in smart-gap mode, after which sequences retaining less than 15% of the alignment columns as non-gap positions were removed. Trees were inferred by maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE v3.1.1 [M9]; substitution models selected with ModelFinder [M10]: Q.PFAM+R5 for the OCD-fold family, JTT+R5 for the GDPD family, JTT+R7 for the cucumopine synthase family). Branch support was assessed using SH-aLRT [M12] and ultrafast bootstrap [M11] (1,000 replicates). As a control, a second alignment of the same dataset was generated with an HMM profile; conclusions were consistent. The trees (Newick, with supports), the trimmed alignments and the IQ-TREE reports of the six family trees summarised in Figure 3 are provided as Data S2 (Supplementary_Data_trees.tar.gz, with a manifest); the full trees are shown in Figures S2–S7.

The final lineage of each locus was defined by its position in the tree, not by the profile-based call: a locus was assigned to the *sus*-like or *cus*-like lineage when it fell within the smallest clade containing all canonical T-DNA references of the gene, and to one of the other lineages when it fell within the clade of that lineage. A group of fungal loci was counted as a lineage of bacterial origin when it groups with bacterial homologs, the alternative placements tested are rejected by the AU test where the test is informative (below), and at least one independent line of evidence supports the placement (a bacterial source locus, nesting within the T-DNA clade, location in islands with other genes of bacterial type, or a patchy distribution among related genomes). Near-identical sequences collapsed before tree inference were assigned the lineage of their representative. Short fragments that could not be placed in the tree are reported separately as assigned by profile only.

*chs*-like genes. The fungal *chs*-like proteins, their best bacterial hits in nr (up to three per genus), the seed proteins of *chs* and *ags* and native fungal homologs of *ags* from different classes were aligned with MAFFT (L-INS-i), trimmed with ClipKIT (smart-gap mode; sequences with <15% non-gap columns removed), and a tree was inferred with IQ-TREE (model Q.PFAM+I+G4 selected by ModelFinder; 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap and SH-aLRT replicates), rooted on the T-DNA *ags* seeds; the family trees of Sus, Cus and GDPD were rooted on their outgroups of distant superfamily members, and the opine dehydrogenase trees on the *ocs* and *vis* seeds.

*odh* island. The fungal *odh* proteins, their best bacterial hits in nr (up to two per genus), the seed proteins of *nos*, *ocs* and *vis*, the opine dehydrogenase-like proteins of *Rhypophila* and *Butyriboletus roseoflavus* and native fungal homologs of the family from seven classes were aligned and analysed as for *chs* (model Q.PFAM+G4).

Nopaline synthase subfamily. The opine dehydrogenase-like proteins of Boletales (Section 2.4) and the three *Rhypophila* proteins were placed in a tree together with the T-DNA nopaline synthases, their non-T-DNA rhizobial paralogs and other bacterial homologs from the reverse searches (up to three per genus), native fungal homologs of the family and the *ocs* and *vis* seeds as outgroup (64 sequences; alignment, trimming and tree inference as for *chs*; model Q.PFAM+R4).

*Pleurotus* GDPD protein. The rule-C GDPD protein of *Pleurotus pulmonarius* (KAF4581365.1) and its miniprot model from *P. placentodes* were added to the GDPD-family dataset (269 sequences) together with the best bacterial hit (*Ensifer* sp. Root31, WP_156585617.1) and five native GDPD proteins of Agaricomycetes from nr (*Psilocybe*, *Lactarius* ×2, *Trametes*, *Rhizopogon*). The 277 sequences were aligned to the GDPD profile HMM with hmmalign (HMMER v3.4; match columns only), trimmed with ClipKIT v2.7.0 (smart-gap; sequences with <15% non-gap columns removed; 176 sequences retained), and trees were inferred with IQ-TREE v3.1.1 under Q.PFAM+G4 and, as a control, VT+I+R4 (1,000 ultrafast bootstrap and SH-aLRT replicates).

The approximately unbiased (AU) test [M13] compares the likelihood of the unconstrained tree with trees in which a specific relationship is forcibly imposed (e.g., "fungal genes must form one branch with non-T-DNA bacterial genes"). For each such hypothesis, the best tree under that constraint was constructed, and p-values were computed from 10,000 pseudoreplicates: p < 0.05 — the hypothesis is rejected by the data.

Hypotheses were formulated separately for each lineage, because in the GDPD and cucumopine synthase families the fungal genes fall into several independent groups. For each lineage the constraints forced the fungal genes to group (i) with the T-DNA references of the gene, (ii) with other bacterial homologs (chromosomal or catabolic rhizobial genes, actinobacterial-type and *mis* genes, other bacterial opine dehydrogenases or M24 aminopeptidases), (iii) with the native fungal homologs of the family and (iv) with each other (monophyly of the fungal genes, as expected after a single acquisition without later exchange). In addition, ten constraints placed the fungal *chs*-like genes (all eleven, or the ten *Fusarium* genes alone) next to each of the five Ti-plasmid *chs* genes, to ask whether they are nested within the plasmid clade; for the *cus*-like genes outside Boletales (16 sequences of 10 species, on the alignment and ML tree of the Cus family), placement with the T-DNA references excluding the Boletales genes was tested, and the number of independent acquisitions was counted as the minimal number of groups consisting only of these genes in the ML tree, before and after collapsing nodes with SH-aLRT < 80 or UFBoot < 95; for the *Rhypophila* genes, grouping with the two adjacent native opine dehydrogenases of Sordariomycetes, with all native homologs of Sordariomycetes and with all native fungal homologs was tested. The constrained searches were followed by AU tests with 10,000 RELL replicates. All 60 constraints, with their composition and p-values, are listed in Table S11 and Figure S8, with the biological alternative tested by each constraint, the leaf sets and the admissible conclusion in Table S11b and the constraint trees and search outputs in Data S2b; because several hypotheses were tested per family, the p-values were also adjusted by the Holm procedure within each family, which left every verdict unchanged. The count of *cus*-like groups outside Boletales is the minimum number of fungal-only clades on the gene tree after collapsing weakly supported nodes; it equals the number of acquisitions only if the copies were not exchanged among fungi or transferred back to bacteria.

Three kinds of evidence are kept apart. (i) The support of the node that groups the fungal genes with their bacterial relatives (SH-aLRT/UFBoot) measures how well the tree resolves that grouping (Table S18). (ii) The AU test rejects, or fails to reject, specific alternative placements; each verdict concerns only the alternative that was constrained (Table S11b). (iii) Constraints that force the candidates together with all native fungal members of a family are informative only where that native set is itself monophyletic in the unconstrained tree; where it is not (the OCD-fold, GDPD and opine dehydrogenase families; in the GDPD family the acc-type and pKerr14a-type native members are separate), rejection of the native constraint is not by itself evidence against a native origin (constraints SUS_nat-3/4, ACS-4, ACS_nat-7, NOS_nat-4/5, NOS_rh-7/8, ODH-1/2/3 in Table S11b), and the conclusion rests on (i), on the constraints among bacterial groups and on the genomic context.

*Structural phylogeny.* As an independent line of evidence, structural phylogenetics was performed for 274 proteins of the GDPD family (fungal genes, T-DNA references and bacterial homologs). Three-dimensional structures were predicted with ColabFold v1.6.2 (AlphaFold2) [M14, M15]; FoldMason [M16] generated a structural multiple alignment from the coordinates and the 3Di structural alphabet, and IQ-TREE built a tree from this alignment, which was used to check the monophyly of the acc-type lineage, the pKerr14a-type lineage and the T-DNA references (Figure S9).

### 2.7. Genomic context and islands

For each locus we delimited a context window along the contig. Moving outwards from the locus in both directions, we took the first gene on each side that looks like an ordinary gene of the carrier genome — a multi-exon gene whose best homologs in GenBank nr are fungal — and called it an "anchor". The region between the two anchors was called an island when it contains more than one gene; if the anchors are the immediate neighbours of the locus, the locus is reported as a single gene. The window describes the local organisation of the locus, not the boundaries of the transferred DNA. In islands, all genes between the anchors were classified by their best homologs in nr as fungal, bacterial-like, or mobile elements, and the GC content of the island was calculated.

To test whether the island is an insertion, the two anchors were searched (miniprot) in genomes of close relatives lacking the gene: if the anchors are adjacent there and the island is absent, this indicates an insertion.

Proteins encoded in 17 islands (163 proteins) were additionally compared with the plasmid catalogue (DIAMOND, E < 10⁻⁵) to identify other genes of plasmid origin and to compare gene order with that of T-regions. For the *odh* island of *F. poae*, the proteins within 50 kb of each copy were searched against the plasmid catalogue in the same way, and the locus of the best bacterial hit of the island proteins was examined in the annotated genome of *Agrobacterium larrymoorei* SORGH_AS_0974 (GCF_031453325.1).

### 2.8. Compositional analysis

For the 75 intact genes of four lineages — sus-like (34 loci), cus-like (14 in Boletales and 20 outside), acc-type GDPD (3) and pKerr14a-type acs-like (4); two sus-like and two pKerr14a-type coding sequences were excluded because of ambiguous bases or a length that is not a multiple of three — and for the nos-subfamily opine dehydrogenase genes of Boletales (six loci, the gene itself excluded from the background), the GC content of the coding sequence, the GC content at third codon positions (GC3) and relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) were compared with those of carrier genes. The background consisted of all coding sequences of the genome for annotated assemblies and, for unannotated assemblies, of the genes predicted in the regions surrounding the loci; for the 55 loci of Boletales a pooled background of 1,001 genes was used in addition, because the own-genome background was adequate (at least 30 genes) for only 24 of the 75 loci (five of them in Boletales). Genes of mobile elements, coding sequences shorter than 300 nt and the loci themselves were excluded. For each gene we determined the percentile of its GC3 within the background distribution and the distance of its codon usage from that of the background (one minus the Pearson correlation of RSCU vectors), which was compared with the distances of background genes of similar length. GC3 of the *odh* island genes was compared with the distribution over the 14,418 annotated genes of *F. poae* Fp133, and GC of *chs*-like genes with that of the *Fusarium* carrier genomes.

### 2.9. Test for selection (dN/dS)

To assess whether the acquired genes remain under selective constraint, we calculated the ratio of non-synonymous to synonymous substitution rates (dN/dS, or ω). A ratio well below 1 is consistent with purifying selection on the protein, which indicates conservation but does not establish present-day enzymatic activity. For the non-redundant intact fungal genes in the T-DNA clades (*sus*-like: 31 of the 32 non-redundant intact sequences of the tree, identical copies collapsed, one coding sequence excluded because it did not match its protein; *cus*-like: all 27), codon alignments were built (MAFFT on proteins, back-translated to codons), and trees were inferred using IQ-TREE (GTR+G). The PAML v4.10.7 [M22] codeml program was used to fit models M0 (a single ω for the entire tree), M7 and M8 (to test for sites under positive selection, using a likelihood ratio test), and yn00 [M23] was used for pairwise dN/dS estimations. The analysis was run on both a strict alignment (codon columns with no gaps) and a more complete one (columns with ≤20% gaps) to ensure robustness.

The same analysis was applied to the intact copies of the remaining lineages (Table S15): chs-like genes (6 CDS), cus-like genes outside Boletales (20 CDS of 12 species, and one copy per species), pKerr14a-type acs-like genes (6), nos-subfamily opine dehydrogenase genes of Boletales (4) and the three genes of the odh island (*odh* 5, *pepP* 2, *orf3* 3; M0, M7/M8 and the M8a null model of M8 with ω fixed at 1). The CDS of the Boletales nos-subfamily genes were taken from miniprot gene models, because the catalogue coordinates (blastx HSP bounds) contain stop codons and frameshifts. The set of 27 cus-like CDS analysed first includes 13 of the 20 intact cus-like genes outside Boletales and therefore represents all cus-like genes rather than Boletales alone. yn00 itself removes every alignment column that contains a gap, so the '≤20% gaps' alignment affects only the codeml estimates; for yn00 on that alignment we used pairwise deletion of gaps (script yn00_pairwise_loose.py). Where synonymous sites were saturated (median pairwise dS > 2, cus-like genes outside Boletales) we relied on the M0 estimate rather than on pairwise yn00 values, and the median over pairs with dS ≤ 2 is reported in addition. The M0 estimates pool branches before and after transfer and are therefore averages over bacterial and fungal evolution; no branch model was fitted.

Sensitivity to the integration status (2026-10-13). Four sets were recomputed after excluding the loci whose integration is uncertain (Section 2.5): cus-like, Boletales, confirmed or supported only (7 genes; M0 ω 0.181, 0.213 on the ≤20%-gap alignment); the same with one gene per species (4 genes; 0.113, 0.142); pKerr14a-type acs-like, confirmed or supported only (4 genes; 0.617, 0.642; M8a versus M8 p = 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ with six BEB sites > 0.95, treated as exploratory because of the four barely diverged sequences and 181 codons); nos-subfamily ODH, Boletales, confirmed or supported only (3 genes; 0.786, 0.807; star tree, M0 only). The sus-like set (16 of its 31 sequences from uncertain loci) and the cus-like set outside Boletales (1 of 20) were not filtered. All codon alignments, trees and codeml/yn00 files are in Data S4 (MANIFEST.tsv lists set, file and description).

### 2.10. Expression analysis (RNA-seq)

To determine whether the acquired genes are transcribed, we searched the SRA [M26] for RNA-seq data of the carrier genera. For Boletales, the only datasets found were for *Boletus edulis* (SRR9650682, SRR9650679) and *Butyriboletus subappendiculatus* (ERR13363456); neither isolate is one of the sequenced carriers and no genome of either isolate is available. Reads were mapped with splicing awareness (minimap2) to the carrier assembly of the same species (*B. edulis* GCA_038093215.1; 94–97% of the reads mapped) or of the closest carrier (*B. roseoflavus* GCA_019364595.1; 89%), and read counts at the loci (primary alignments with mapping quality ≥ 1) were compared with the median read count of the genes of the genome. No reads mapped to any locus, which cannot be told from the absence of the genes in the RNA-seq isolates (analysis/expression/expression_summary.tsv; Table S16).

For *chs*-like genes, transcription was examined in nine public RNA-seq datasets of *F. vanettenii* 77-13-4 and *F. oxysporum* f. sp. *lycopersici* 4287 (Table S16): 10 million read pairs per dataset were mapped to the genome with minimap2 in spliced mode, and reads overlapping the exons of each annotated gene were counted and normalised to gene length and to the total over all genes. For the *odh* island, transcription was examined in 15 public RNA-seq datasets of the carrier strain *F. poae* Fp133 (BioProject PRJNA924782; Table S16): the first 10 million reads of each run were mapped to the Fp133 assembly (GCA_046056315.1) with minimap2 in spliced mode and counted as above. Only primary alignments with mapping quality ≥ 1 were counted, so reads that map equally well to several copies of the *odh* island (mapping quality 0) were not counted and cannot be assigned to a specific copy; transcription is therefore shown for the gene family of the island at its annotated copies, not for a specific copy.

### 2.11. Species clusters of Boletales

For each lineage, species and genomes in which the gene was detected were recorded, separately for intact copies and pseudogenes. Species assignment of assemblies was verified by genome-wide similarity, because NCBI labels proved unreliable.

Relatedness of assemblies was evaluated with Mash v2.3 [M17] (sketch size 20,000, k = 21) — a rapid comparison of k-mer sets giving an average nucleotide identity (ANI) estimate; assemblies with ANI >97% (Mash distance <0.03) were grouped into operational genome clusters (species-level clusters), defined as connected components and called "species clusters" (358 clusters for the 517 Boletales assemblies).
